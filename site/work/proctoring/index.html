AI-proctored
exam

Edge AI on an Android phone that watches gaze, head pose, sound and typing, and locks the device down while the exam runs.

Manuscript in preparation for IEEE submission. Java, Android SDK, ML Kit, Firebase, CameraX.

Problem

A live exam can leak through several channels at once. A candidate can look away, get help from someone nearby, type answers that look scripted, or leave the exam app. A system that watches only one of those misses the rest.

I wanted a proctor that reads several signals together and runs on the phone itself.

Approach

Treat the phone as a bundle of sensors. The camera gives gaze and head pose, the microphone gives sound level, an ultrasonic band gives proximity, the keyboard gives timing, and the operating system gives lockdown.

Each signal is modest on its own. The value is in combining them, which is why the pipeline is built around all five.

Technical
breakdown

  1. Vision and audio

    ML Kit BlazeFace and CameraX feed a real-time pipeline that tracks gaze, head pose and RMS audio level. It runs at 22 fps with inference latency under 120 ms.

  2. Collusion and scripted input

    An ultrasonic proximity engine (FFT over the 20 to 30 kHz band) and a keystroke-variance analyzer flag collusion and scripted input. Together they reduced the missed-detection rate by 35%.

  3. Device lockdown

    Android Kiosk Mode (startLockTask), FLAG_SECURE and clipboard sanitization enforce lockdown at the OS level and remove common cheating vectors during live exams.

  4. Live data to native UI

    A MatrixCursor bridge binds asynchronous Firebase Realtime Database streams to native UI adapters, which cut UI refresh latency by 45%.

Stack

Java, Android SDK, ML Kit (BlazeFace), CameraX, Firebase Realtime Database.
